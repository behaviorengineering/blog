Denoising reality

Your brain doesn't just wait for data; it runs a continuous prediction loop. It produces a best-guess model of what is out there, then uses sensory input to correct the parts that are wrong.

What if everything you are seeing, hearing, and feeling is not actually reality? It is a controlled hallucination your brain constructs.

It is like the brain gets "poked" by sensory neurons and immediately generates a model to explain the disturbance. It then repeatedly checks how that prediction matches the rest of the incoming information, using its priors (past learnings and experience) to effectively denoise reality until the model and the senses align.

That flips the usual story. Senses are not the source of a world model. They are the error signal that keeps the model honest.

Why illusions are a clue

If perception were a passive readout, illusions would be weird bugs. Under predictive processing, they are expected: the brain commits to the explanation that best fits its priors and the noisy data it is getting.

Your brain faces a dilemma: sensory evidence suggests a hollow shape, but this violates its strong prior belief that faces protrude outward.

Like the mask illusion, your brain also has a very low prior for finding a real tiger in the office (other than those in suits), so it will likely explain away a striped orange glimpse as a reflection or a poster first.
