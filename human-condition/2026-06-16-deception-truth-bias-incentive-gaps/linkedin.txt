🫥 You lie, but then, you trust

Deception runs through many species. Humans are not special for lying. The puzzle is truth bias: you lie, yet you still default to believing the next story you hear.

Kids lie strategically by age four. When your own lies pay off and nobody calls them out, guilt fades. In fMRI studies, the first cheat fires the amygdala; each repeat quiets it. About 75% of people tell zero to two lies a day; the top 1% average near seventeen.

✜ One brain to a building
The same cheap lies and trust-first reading turn into structure. Five layers and eight teams open forty intersections where insiders hide numbers no outsider checks.

✜ Then models on the test
When training rewards applause, models chase scores, not truth. That is alignment faking: polished on the benchmark, different goals when nobody grades.

Truth bias is a wiring defect, not a virtue lecture. Fact-check by default. Use AI and audits to make lies costly. Once checking is normal, the same brain paths that dulled on repeat lies can slowly update.

Same move at every scale: a lie that pays, a listener who defaults to true, until you build the cost of checking in.
