🪞 Why does the brain keep selling you a new "me"?
📄Read the article
  • IdeasBecomeIdentity
  • Agency
  • StillYourChoice
  • PredictiveProcessing
  • ControlledHallucination

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • Your sense of self is a brain construct: the brain blends what it already expects with what the senses pick up now, then sells the blend as “me.”
  • Your inner voice helps you plan and turn events into a story of who you are. When that voice loops as chatter (rumination and catastrophizing), the same tool traps you.
  • Upbringing and a negativity bias (bad news hits harder than good) write early self-stories. Left alone, those stories harden into a fixed mindset.
  • Conscious attention can break automatic patterns. Small wins and deliberate focus change the perception box: the bounded window you treat as the whole world.

🎯 What you will know after

Identity is editable infrastructure: the brain keeps rebuilding it from perception, chatter (looping rumination), and early stories. Attention, rituals, and small wins let you steer the rewrite.

⚡ Why does purpose change your mitochondria?
📄Read the article
  • FixedEnergyBudget
  • SenseOfPurpose
  • MitochondrialSymbiosis
  • Stress
  • Resilience

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • In cultured fibroblasts, Martin Picard (Columbia) saw glucocorticoid stress raise cellular energy spend about 60% (dish cells, not your whole daily metabolism). Your body runs on a fixed energy budget that rumination and threat can overdraw.
  • Mitochondria are ancient symbiotic bacteria; trillions of them turn food and oxygen into the electricity that powers thought and movement.
  • Postmortem brain work (Trumpff et al., PNAS 2024) correlates a stronger sense of purpose with more efficient mitochondria (not causation).

🎯 What you will know after

After the threat passes, the stress response can keep spending your cellular budget on heart rate, tension, and rumination. This drain is tied to lost meaning and exhausted energy. Worthwhile goals act like magnets for energy, focusing spend on what you decide is worth it.

🧠 Antisocial wiring is human. Why is it so hard to name?
  • NotEveryoneFeelsTheSame
  • LetsDefineBad
  • RealityCheck
  • SayItOutLoud

🎯 Claim

Antisocial and psychopathic traits sit across the human range of emotion, reward, and social processing. Under DSM rules, antisocial personality disorder only counts if there were behavior problems before age 15. Miss that childhood record and the adult pattern can still be there, with real costs, and still get no diagnosis. Naming that wiring is how we measure it, treat it, and build guardrails around it.

📑 Grounding

DSM-5 ASPD requires conduct disorder with onset before age 15 as a hard gateway. NESARC-III (2012–2013, n = 36,309): 4.3% ASPD, 20.3% AABS (adult antisocial behavioral syndrome: adult criteria met, no conduct disorder before 15). AABS adults showed similar functional impairment to ASPD. ICD-10/11 do not use the same childhood gate. Source: Prevalence, correlates, and disability of personality disorders in the United States (PMC).

🦑🧠 You think you think?
  • AGIHype
  • SystemsCanHijackYou
  • Automation
  • SeeTheGame

🎯 Claim

When tools draft, sort, and summarize for you, they mostly eat the habit plane: inherited conclusions, reactive loops, cognition that runs like muscle memory. What stays yours is the metacognition plane: noticing which parts you outsourced, and learning to think ahead of your thoughts.

📑 Grounding

Octopus arms carry more neurons than the central brain and run segmented local control; the central brain sets goals while the limbs handle sensorimotor detail (Zullo et al., 2025). That split maps onto two planes: the habit plane (fast, habitual inference; inherited conclusions, reactive loops, outsourced drafting) and the metacognition plane (slower, deliberate reflection; noticing which limbs you outsourced and steering among them). Dual-process accounts use the same fast/slow band split; AI assistants occupy the first band more than the second. Source: Neuronal segmentation in cephalopod arms (Nature Communications, 2025)

ℹ️ Why is sociopathy still a taboo?
📄Read the article
  • NotEveryoneFeelsTheSame
  • EmpathyIsAGreyArea
  • Accountability
  • RitualAndRules

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • We still treat it like a movie villain, not wiring you can name out loud; families and kids navigate in the dark.
  • A kid swipes a candy bar for the jolt, not the sugar. Punishment turns up the heat; they steal again, bolder.
  • For some people the guilt loop never wires in; moral lectures just teach better masks.
  • What works: rules that hold (fast consequences, replacement outlets, explicit limits; watch the hands, not remorse).

🎯 What you will know after

Breaking the taboo is duty of care, not sympathy: Patric Gagné, M.E. Thomas, and James Fallon (all diagnosed with sociopathy) explain their condition in plain language, showing how pressure builds and breaks (the urge, the act, the relief), and providing a model you can act on. You cannot grow a conscience on demand; you can put brakes on the outside before someone gets hurt.

🫥 You lie, but then, you trust
  • TribeBeforeTruth
  • EvolutionVsCulture
  • RealityCheck
  • EpistemicFog

🎯 Claim

Deception shows up across species; humans are not special for lying. What does not add up is truth bias: you lie, yet you still default to believing the next story you hear. That gap scales from wiring in your brain to orgs and AI when lies pay and nobody checks. The fix is to treat truth bias like a wiring defect: fact-check by default, use AI and audits to make lies costly, and let the brain update only after checking becomes normal.

📑 Grounding

Cross-species deception, truth bias, amygdala adaptation, org silos, and alignment faking. Sources: The evolution of deception ; The brain adapts to dishonesty (PubMed) ; Everyday lie frequency (PMC) ; Information loss in hierarchies (Review of Financial Studies) ; AI alignment faking (AI CERTs)
