🪞 Why does the brain keep selling you a new "me"?

Ever notice how a bad day makes the whole room look worse? Same kitchen, same people, and suddenly every comment is proof that you are failing.

A short Big Think clip with Heather Berlin, Ethan Kross, and Nicole Vignola says that feeling of "me" is a live mix. The brain blends what it already expects with what your senses pick up now, then sells you the blend as who you are.

The voice in your head is useful. It helps you plan. Then it can loop: rumination, worst-case movies, the same argument on repeat. That chatter is the planning tool stuck in a groove.

A lot of the harsh self-talk started as copying. You watched how parents and friends talked about themselves, and the brain kept the pattern because bad news sticks harder than good news.

The hopeful bit: if it is built in real time, you can name the box they put you in (and the one you put yourself in) and rewrite it.
