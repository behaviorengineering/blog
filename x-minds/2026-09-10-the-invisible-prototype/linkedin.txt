The Invisible Prototype

For decades, autism diagnosis treated young boys with overt, disruptive behavior as the universal yardstick. Girls and women with the same wiring often pass the filter: quiet, internalized patterns get read as quirks, not autism.

✜ Boys became the blueprint
In the 1920s, Grunya Sukhareva described autism in both boys and girls. Later work by Kanner and Asperger built a male-dominated canon. Asperger's influential sample was four boys. Autism hardened into a boy's experience, and female presentations fell out of view.

✜ Jack gets flagged; Chloe gets "quirks"
Classroom filters often need loud disruption to trigger an assessment. Identical repetitive or intense patterns in a boy named Jack get flagged. In a girl named Chloe, the same patterns become personality quirks or social eccentricity. High-visibility male-coded signals pass; quiet presentations slip through.

✜ Camouflage buys belonging and spends the self
Many autistic girls construct a persona to reduce social friction: the "wrong" My Little Pony toys bought on purpose just to stay in the peer group. Masking helps her navigate the room. The cost is a lifelong disconnect from her own needs, then exhaustion when the performance outruns her capacity.

When tools only catch loud disruption, quiet compensation looks like the absence of autism. Ask of any checklist: does it measure the condition, or how loudly the condition shows?
