🧩 The structural refusal of a shared timeline

You put a date or an intent on the table, and the fight goes to chaos instead of closing. This is less a normal disagreement than two ways of seeing the same scene. One side thinks facts clear things up; the other treats that clarity as something to dodge.

✜ Character pathology
Palmatier names the pattern: how someone handles reality and blame. Clinic labels often miss half the behavior or over-label the person. This name points at the habit first. When they refuse the record, facts will not close the fight, and the stated dispute is mostly cover.

✜ Fact as invasion
In a normal conflict, a fact clears what is unknown. Here it leaves a trail of rewritten stories. For a false self that cannot survive a mistake, that trail is an inconvenient continuity. When a fact in time proves the error, the floor drops out, and they attack whoever put them on the record.

Feeling becomes proof: "I feel attacked by the facts, so you must be attacking me." They bend reality around the feeling, turn your tone into "evidence," attribute their own defects to you, and drop the facts to focus on their reaction. Rage at an irrefutable fact is fear of losing control of the narrative. The loop only needs to last long enough for accountability to dissolve into a fog of complexity.

You match dates; they build a negotiable reality where truth is optional. Every valid clarification becomes material for the next rewrite.

If you cannot agree what happened, you cannot move on. Better to avoid contact and set limits than burn time on heroic conversations. Any bond that asks you to drop checkable facts for "peace" is broken. That "peace" which denies reality does not hold.
