⚓ Neutrality is a risky ride

Under pressure, neutrality turns into drift, and drift makes you steerable.

If you do not take a stance, the system nudges you into the roles it rewards: bystander, recruit, or enforcer.

✜ The Bystander: "Not my problem." You wait to see what others do, so the crowd quietly steers you.

✜ The Recruit: "These people see the real pattern." Belonging and status pull you in, so you adopt the group's script and call harm "loyalty."

✜ The Enforcer: "I am just doing my duty." You act under authority, shift into agent mode (Milgram's agentic state), and wash your hands of the outcome.

Systems love this. They use ambiguity, hierarchy, and slow escalation so refusal feels disloyal, the same pattern bystander-effect research describes when everyone waits for a signal.

✜ Captain mode sets non-negotiables, treats authority as input, and owns outcomes.
✜ Vessel mode mirrors the crowd, lets roles define ethics, and trades doubt for belonging.

Before you slide into a role, hit the brakes: what outcome am I actually building, and what evidence would change my mind?
