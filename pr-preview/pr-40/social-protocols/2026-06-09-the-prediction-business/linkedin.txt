🔮 The prediction business sells obedience as foresight

Carissa Véliz on The Tea: democracies now run surveillance stacks East Germany could only afford for a third of the population, and we still treat many algorithmic lines as weather reports.

✜ Surveillance is the fuel
Phones and platforms collect thousands of signals per person. Mass data buys prediction and influence: who will click, vote, default, or protest. "Nothing to hide" is about limiting what power can infer about you, not proving innocence.

✜ Scores act on bets
AI scores for hiring, loans, and health look neutral. They are bets on your behavior that institutions execute. The label becomes the lever.

✜ Predictions are speech acts
A model that "predicts" you will fail reads like fact and acts like instruction. Executive prophecy ("you will all use AI tomorrow") recruits the public to fulfill a preferred timeline. That is obedience in advance.

✜ Hype hides the now
Headlines about existential AI doom distract from immediate threats to privacy, accountability, and anonymous protest. Medical scores and chatbots become shields when institutions want to deny responsibility (Air Canada tried to blame the grief-fee bot).

Models trained on the past can miss systemic risk. Spot the prescription. Use privacy tools, stay skeptical, protect time offline.
