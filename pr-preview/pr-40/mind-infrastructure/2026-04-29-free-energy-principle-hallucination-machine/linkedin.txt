🧠🔮 Your brain is a controlled hallucination

Your brain is a prediction machine instead of a passive camera. "Reality" is the brain's strongest guess, optimized to minimize prediction errors instead of showing objective truth.

✜ Instead of waiting for data, your brain runs a continuous prediction loop. It generates a best-guess model of the world and uses sensory input to correct errors.

✜ This flips the usual story: senses act as the error signal that keeps the model honest instead of being the source of the world model.

✜ Passive perception would treat optical illusions as weird bugs. Under predictive processing, they are expected. They are strong priors in action, generating hallucinations when sensory data is noisy, ambiguous, or novel.

✜ For example: your brain assigns a very low prior to finding a real tiger in the office. It will likely explain away a striped orange glimpse as a reflection or a poster first.

When sensory data and prior beliefs are out of balance, it creates tension. Neuroscientists call this free energy. The brain's algorithm is one continuous loop: predict, compare, update, and repeat.

This video explores how your brain works and why it effectively lives in a simulation. It provides the core loop and the vocabulary to go deeper: priors, prediction error, and the recognition vs generative split.
