The Pain That Looks Like Personality

A partner puts a period in a text instead of an emoji, and shame hits your chest like a fist. Heart racing. Mind collapsing into worthlessness. That spike has a name: Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD). The brake that should slow the surge never arrives.

✜ Misread as character
After a devastating breakup, a teenager spirals into self-harm and gets labeled with a personality disorder. Shame piles on. When ADHD comes into view, the story shifts: the prefrontal cortex is missing inhibitory signals. This is a brain without a natural pause, not a broken personality.

✜ Wrong chemistry, wrong pause
Treatment-resistant depression and anxiety often hide a dopamine and noradrenaline gap. Spot ADHD-linked RSD and options like Guanfacine become available: they boost noradrenergic activity, create an emotional pause, and calm an overactive fear response. When the spikes finally quiet, you see a neurochemical gap, not a permanent mood sentence.

✜ Name the spike
A vague "let's talk Monday" from a boss can burn an entire weekend. The brain fails to rationalize in real time. Say "this is my RSD" and you open a gap. In that gap, best-case and realistic outcomes can land before the landslide.

Stop asking if you are unstable. Ask whether you get even one second between trigger and spike. When the wave hits, name it. That label is the artificial pause your cortex needs to start thinking again.
