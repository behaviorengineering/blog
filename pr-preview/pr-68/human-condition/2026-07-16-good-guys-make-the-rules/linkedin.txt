😇 Good guys make the rules

We can cheat, because we are the good guys.

After you prove you look fair, the next shortcut gets cheaper if you can rename it and still call yourself clean. You can even invent that proof right before you need it.

✜ Look fair, then cheat →
Reject a sexist statement. Next, pick the man for a job labeled male. Monin and Miller found that sequence made the bias easier to live with.

Brown's team asked people how generous they would be on soft charity dilemmas, then put them on a math screen that shows the answer unless you hit a key in time. People who had just rated their own virtue "forgot" that key more than people who rated an acquaintance. That is moral credentialing.

✜ Rename the cheat →
"I've proved I'm fair, so this one tilt is fine." Cheating becomes fair compensation. Prejudice becomes fit. A grab becomes being practical. That rewrite is moral rationalization.

✜ Invent the halo →
You do not always need a real good deed. Invent one on the way in, or borrow the group's badge: we are the defenders of X, so our rough tactics are justified.

ℹ The badge is not magic. One second to hide the answer: "I was too slow" still works. Ten seconds: the same miss looks deliberate, and the virtue rating barely moves people.

We write the rulebook, then the fine print only we get to read. Orgs help by splitting one dirty decision across many hands, and by calling it creative accounting or flexible policy.

Our game is your game, but not ours.
Thick rulebook for you.
Blank for us.
