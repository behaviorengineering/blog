🧵 Built to last: how systems puppet themselves

Durable systems rarely need to hold a whip. They make people want to pull the strings, because belonging and purpose feel good. That is how institutions get collaboration at scale without constant force.

✜ They set a goal you can chase forever but never finish.
✜ They turn that goal into a lived world: rules for what "good" looks like, stories that make the rules noble, and rituals that make the story feel real.
✜ They pay in human currency: status, meaning, and belonging. The submission feels like participation.

When the system fails, it protects the ideal by blaming the "interpreter": a bad leader, a corrupt priesthood, or a misguided manager. The core promise is never at fault.

We see this everywhere. If a corporate mission burns people out, it was just "bad management." If enterprise software fails to deliver, it was the "low-level users who didn't adopt it correctly."

The AI industry has built its own version. The narrative of inevitable AGI justifies a massive concentration of power today. The goal stays perpetually around the corner, and any failure is blamed on the hardware or the tuning, never the vision of superintelligence.

The technology is new, but the protocol is as old as the first campfire. The machine survives because the group develops a collective immune response to protect the flame.
