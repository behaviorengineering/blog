🧠 Why does a healthy brain forget your keys?
📄Read the article
  • RealityCheck
  • Stress
  • CognitiveCapacity
  • StillYourChoice

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • Lost keys and blank names usually mean you never paid enough attention for the brain to encode the moment into a stored memory.
  • Most everyday lapses are distraction at that moment. You cannot retrieve a parking spot you never looked at.
  • Alzheimer’s can run silently for years in the hippocampus, the region that binds sensory pieces into new memories. Early signs are failing to form new memories and not knowing how you arrived.
  • For most people, sleep, exercise, diet, and new learning change whether amyloid plaques, sticky protein clumps that build over 15 to 20 years, ever reach a tipping point.

🎯 What you will know after

Everyday encoding failures and Alzheimer’s look alike from the outside and run on different mechanisms. While amyloid plaques build for 15 to 20 years in the hippocampus, most people can still change the odds with sleep, movement, diet, and cutting unmanaged chronic stress.

🤝 Why does conquest start with a partnership?
📄Read the article
  • SeeTheGame
  • PowerAndControl
  • RiskExport
  • CheapNarratives

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • Rome, in Johnathan Bi’s reading of Machiavelli, conquered by inviting neighbors as junior partners: they kept their laws and rulers, and they fought the next wars as proxies.
  • Fraud did the scaling: Rome used those partners to take the next cities, then turned the partners into subjects once the work was done.
  • A pyramid sales scheme runs the same invite at human scale: you keep your name and pitch, recruit the next ring, and the people above you live off that expansion.

🎯 What you will know after

Keep your laws, logo, or title while your people fight someone else’s next campaign. After the work is done, the partner becomes the subject: city-states ran that play, and pyramid sales schemes still do.

🪞 Why does the brain keep selling you a new "me"?
📄Read the article
  • IdeasBecomeIdentity
  • Agency
  • StillYourChoice
  • PredictiveProcessing
  • ControlledHallucination

🎬 What you probably do not know yet

  • Your sense of self is a brain construct: the brain blends what it already expects with what the senses pick up now, and you take that mix as who you are.
  • Your inner voice helps you plan and turn events into a story of who you are. When that voice loops as chatter (rumination and catastrophizing), you replay the same worry without solving anything.
  • Upbringing and a negativity bias (bad news hits harder than good) write early self-stories. Left alone, you keep using those stories as an adult.
  • Conscious attention can break automatic patterns. Small wins and deliberate focus change the perception box: the bounded window you treat as the whole world.

🎯 What you will know after

The brain keeps rebuilding identity from perception, looping chatter, and early stories. Attention, rituals, and small wins change what gets rebuilt.
