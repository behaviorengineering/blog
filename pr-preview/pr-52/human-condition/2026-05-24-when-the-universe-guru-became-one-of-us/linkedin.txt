When the universe guru became one of us

Michio Kaku spends most of a Diary of a CEO interview on multiverses and quantum hype. One Vietnam story is where he stops sounding like a guru.

✜ Candy was not candy
At grenade training, recruits asked their sergeant about scars on one side of his face and neck. He told them about a boy: "Candy, candy." It was a hand grenade. Half the sergeant's body took shrapnel.

✜ Why would a boy do that?
Kaku's answer: because he believed in something. Not random evil. A story strong enough to turn a gift into a trap. His lesson stuck: believe in the goodness of men and that men can do evil. Then fight for what you think is right.

✜ The song at 4 a.m.
It was not just "we're number one, we're going to win this war." Every morning at 4 a.m. he sang about killing "Charlie Kong." Then he asked himself whether he was on the right side or the wrong side.

Belief is not abstract in war. It is a child with a grenade and a soldier who once sang the team song before breakfast.
