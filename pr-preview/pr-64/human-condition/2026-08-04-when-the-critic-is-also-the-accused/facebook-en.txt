🪞 Ever notice how some people (or even we ourselves) get way too angry at things others do? 

It turns out there's a tested mechanism for that "high-heat" judgment. When we try to hide a trait in ourselves that we don't like, we often start seeing it everywhere else. It's called "defensive projection." 

Research shows that if you try to bury an unwanted trait, it stays active in the back of your mind. This "mental availability" then biases how you read others, making you unusually quick to see that same fault in everyone else. 

By condemning it in someone else, we create a boundary that keeps the trait from feeling like our own. It provides a temporary sense of relief (a way to convince ourselves that the problem belongs to someone else). 

Next time your anger outlasts the provocation, stop for a second. Ask what that heat is protecting: a real observation about them, or a part of yourself you haven't wanted to face?
