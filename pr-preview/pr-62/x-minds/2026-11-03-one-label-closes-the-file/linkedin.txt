A single label is a ceiling rather than a map

A clinician says one word at the school gate, and the relief in the room makes everyone stop asking what else the child struggles with. The teacher plans the term around attention while movement, speech, and sleep never make it onto the form.

✜ Clinics often match symptoms to one checklist and treat that word as the whole profile. UK NICE expects autism assessments to cover coexisting conditions and motor problems, yet many files still close after the first label that fits.

✜ Local agencies run support in sequence. Funding and referrals attach to the paper that arrived, so a pupil with a language gap and an attention label may get help for focus alone while the next door stays shut for months.

✜ UK research puts developmental language disorder around one child in thirteen, and mainstream schools often steer more support toward autism than toward language needs at the same severity.

✜ Clinicians who skip head injury history often match focus problems to ADHD while a past injury stays off the chart. NICE treats acquired brain injury as part of that same assessment picture.

✜ The child waiting outside the head teacher's office pays for that one-label model every day. Parents can ask which movement screen and head injury history ran before the label landed. They can also ask the school for one everyday fix, a pencil grip or a seat near the door, while the second check waits.

The word sets the ceiling for what staff will measure. The child still walks in with every gap the first screen skipped.
