⚡ Why does purpose change your mitochondria?

Energy is real. Most of us feel it as the gap between having the agency to change our lives and barely being through the day.

The energy resistance model treats the body as a fixed energy budget. When infection, threat, or rumination flares, repair, brain maintenance, and anti-aging work have to wait in line. Extra food refills the pool, but the daily budget stays fixed.

The drain often comes from the stress response, not the stress itself. In lab tests, human cells exposed to stress signals raised their energy spend by 60%. They aged faster without any physical damage—just the signal that danger might be outside.

That cost is recorded in our biology. Much like tree rings, stressful months leave white bands along the hair shaft. Your scalp holds a timeline of when your cellular budget went overdrawn.

A sense of purpose acts as a magnet for that energy. Research shows that older adults with a stronger sense of purpose have more efficient mitochondria in their prefrontal cortex. It focuses the spend on what you decide is worth it.

Swap the question "How do I get more energy?" for "Where is my budget going?" When purpose feels foggy and your body feels spent, it is often budget trouble at least as often as mood trouble.
