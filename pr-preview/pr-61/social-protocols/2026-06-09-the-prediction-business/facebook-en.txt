🔮 The prediction business sells obedience as foresight

Ever notice how a "prediction" from a bank, a boss, or a platform feels like a weather report, but it still changes what happens to you?

Carissa Véliz (Oxford) on a long Tea interview: today's democracies collect more signals per person than East Germany could afford for a third of the population. Hiring scores, loan scores, health scores look neutral. They are bets institutions act on.

"Nothing to hide" is not about guilt. It is about limiting what power can infer. When a model says you will fail, or a CEO says you will all use AI tomorrow, that line is often a push dressed up as foresight.

The doom headlines about superintelligence distract from the stuff hitting now: privacy, who is accountable, whether you can protest without a file following you. Medical AI flops and chatbots (remember Air Canada blaming the grief-fee bot) show how "prediction" becomes a shield when someone wants out of responsibility.

Spot the prescription. Skepticism, privacy tools, and time offline still matter.
