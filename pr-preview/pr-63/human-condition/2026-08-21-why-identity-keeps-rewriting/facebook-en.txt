🪞 Why does the brain keep selling you a new "me"?

Ever notice how a bad day makes the whole room look worse? Same kitchen, same people, and suddenly every comment is proof that you are failing.

A short Big Think clip with Heather Berlin, Ethan Kross, and Nicole Vignola says that feeling of "me" is built in real time. The brain blends what it already expects with what your senses pick up now, and you can shift that mix through attention and the story you tell about yourself.

The voice in your head is useful. It helps you plan. Then it can loop: rumination, worst-case movies, the same argument on repeat. That chatter is the same voice replaying a worry without solving anything.

A lot of the harsh self-talk started as copying. You watched how parents and friends talked about themselves, and the brain kept the pattern because bad news sticks harder than good news.

The hopeful bit: because it is built in real time, you can say the story they taught you (and the one you now tell yourself), change what you attend to, and people who know you start meeting a different version of you.
