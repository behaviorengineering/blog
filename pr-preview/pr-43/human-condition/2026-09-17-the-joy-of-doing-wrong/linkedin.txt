The Joy of Doing Wrong

St. Augustine stole pears he did not want, then threw them to the pigs. No hunger. No profit. Just the itch to do something wrong because it was wrong.

That itch is perversity: a sharp claim that you are still unmanageable in a world that keeps trying to predict you.

✜ Petty strikes
Finger in the ice cream. A rule bent for no gain. Same family as the wasted pears. Small, useless moves that privately signal: my will is still mine.

✜ When the map gets too tight
Push too hard to script people and you get reactance. During COVID, some refused the shot because the mandate itself felt like a grab at sovereignty. Less rational on purpose. Territory protected.

✜ High-stakes wrong
Banksy's Girl with Balloon shredded itself at auction. Break the expected move and you can rewrite the game, not just spoil it.

Your small rebellions sit in the same loop. Autonomy is human. Pure reactance is not a plan. You end up defined by what you refuse, not by what you want.

Look at your last act of defiance. Was it aimed at a goal, or just a way to feel unmanageable in front of a rule?

What question can break the autonomy-perversity cycle?
