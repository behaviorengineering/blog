Ever notice how Monday already knows what you are going to do before you have had coffee?

That is the Sm(art) shirt line in a nutshell. If your day is mapped out the second you walk in, you are not living it. You are running a script. Scripts are for systems, not a human day.

If your day repeats step for step, most of it can be automated. That is not an insult to the job. The routine is a closed chain (same steps, same outputs), and your identity does not have to live inside it.

The trap is defending the boring parts because they feel like "who I am at work." Don't defend the routine from automation. Automate it on purpose, so your attention stays for the calls only you can make, the judgment, the awkward human moment.

The old industrial deal trained us to be reliable parts. The next turn is yours: let the machinery run without you in the seat, leave the factory humming, and come back to the parts of you that are not a schedule.
