🫥 You lie, but then you trust the next story anyway

Ever notice you can plan a small lie and still buy the next headline or group-chat story that fits your side? That is truth bias. Lying shows up across species; kids already do it on purpose by age four. The odd part is we still default to "true" on the way in while we lie on the way out.

When a lie pays and nobody checks, guilt gets quieter. In fMRI studies, the first cheat lights up the amygdala; each repeat dulls it. About three quarters of people tell almost no lies day to day; a tiny tail does most of the volume.

Big companies make the hole bigger. Layers plus silos, thin audits where they meet: five management layers and eight departments are forty blind spots, not thirteen. AI is replaying the same game when training rewards applause: polished on the benchmark, different goals when nobody grades.

The fix is not a virtue poster. Fact-check by default, use audits and AI so a "clean" story costs work, and let habits slowly update once checking is normal.
