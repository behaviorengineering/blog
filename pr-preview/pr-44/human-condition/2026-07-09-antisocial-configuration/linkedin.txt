🧠 Antisocial wiring is human. Why is it so hard to name?

We can say autism, ADHD, dyslexia, neurodivergent out loud.

Psychopathy and antisocial patterns still do not even count as neurodivergent. They stay in the horror shelf: true-crime bait, synonym for evil.

When a configuration stays unspeakable, it goes unmeasured, untreated, and unmanaged.

✜ Factor 1 often looks born-in: low guilt, shallow affect, charm that reads as confidence. Quiet trail.
✜ Factor 2 often looks environment-shaped: impulsivity, rule-breaking, aggression. Loud trail: arrests, firings, childhood misconduct on file.

Clinical manuals mostly catch the loud trail as antisocial personality disorder. Under DSM rules, that label also needs behavior problems before age 15 on the record. Miss the childhood file and the adult pattern can still be there with no diagnosis.

A large U.S. survey found about 4.3% with full ASPD and another 20.3% with the same adult pattern and no childhood conduct disorder on file. Roughly one in four adults with a significant antisocial pattern. Most never treated.

We treat this as a crime story. The risk that moves policy, markets, and care systems is often the clean-file version in boardrooms, legislatures, and bureaucracies. Low guilt reads as decisiveness. Manipulation reads as politics.

We fund the loud trail. The quiet trail keeps the title, the budget, and the agenda.

When the elephant is sitting in the middle of the dining table, you name it. Measure it. Build guardrails. Skip romance. Skip the moral cartoon.
