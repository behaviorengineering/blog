🧠 Why can the same ADD medicine ruin one brain and save another?

Diagnosing ADD from symptom checklists alone is like asking a mechanic to fix an engine by listening to the sounds and never opening the hood. Psychiatrists who never look at the brain work the same way. Different activity patterns can look like the same disorder, and a one-size pill leaves many without the attention they require.

✜ Same label, opposite brains
Classic ADD and many inattentive cases show low frontal activity. Stimulants can help that underactive pattern. Overfocused and Ring of Fire patterns sit on the other side: stuck, overactive circuitry. Stimulants can pour fuel on that fire.

✜ Wrong chemistry can look like help
A pastor's wife with inattentive ADD was treated as depression and given Prozac. For her underactive frontal pattern, the drug pushed function lower and tipped into public disinhibition at a stoplight. Imaging, stopping the Prozac, and a targeted stimulant ended the crisis.

What matters is what is happening inside your head, and how you bring that pattern under control: match the plan to the brain you have, not a one-size ADD pill. When meds keep failing, ask for practitioners who use objective brain imaging and build from the scan, not the checklist alone.
