🎭 The good reason is cover for the real one

J. P. Morgan gets credited with a blunt line: a man has two reasons for doing something, a good reason and the real reason.

When you quit a job, take a deal, or pick a side, you usually lead with the version that sounds fair, practical, or kind. That acceptable reason travels well. It lowers conflict, protects reputation, and gives people a story they can nod at without digging.

✜ That outward story is a deniability hood: a clean cover that blocks anyone from pinning the real motive. In a group, it keeps shared suspicion from becoming shared certainty. Inside one skull, it lets you keep acting while the public account stays respectable.

The real push is often messier: status, spite, fear of looking small, control, relief, belonging. Sometimes you know that and still lead with the good reason. Sometimes the motive stays foggy even to you.

✜ Cold-hot empathy gap: when you are calm, you cannot really feel what you will want when you are angry, afraid, or desperate, and vice versa.

You can lie to the room on purpose. You can also buy your own story. Either way, the real push keeps driving. The clean why is what you can say out loud.

When someone hands you a clean why, including when that someone is you, check both sides. What does the good reason shield in public? What does the real reason want when nobody is watching?
