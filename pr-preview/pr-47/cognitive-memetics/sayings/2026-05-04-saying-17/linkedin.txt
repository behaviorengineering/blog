"Ser más agarrado que vieja en moto."

❓ This Venezuelan saying plays on the word "agarrado," meaning both "tight-fisted" and "held on tightly." Imagine an old woman clinging to a motorcycle for dear life; that's how some people cling to their money. It's an absurd, funny image. You use it to call someone extremely stingy or a real penny-pincher, especially when they're comically reluctant to spend or share. It's a playful tease among friends.

AND:
This saying offers a humorous social commentary on generosity. It subtly reinforces cultural values around sharing and reciprocity within Venezuelan society. You'll often hear it used informally among friends or family, perhaps teasing someone who always "forgets" their wallet or is hesitant to chip in for a group meal.
