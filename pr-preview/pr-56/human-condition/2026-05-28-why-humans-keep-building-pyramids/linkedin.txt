🏢🔺 Why does every group organize into a pyramid?

Families get elders. Classrooms get teachers. Companies get managers. Even flat online groups grow moderators and inner circles.

Culture and greed matter, but the pull starts in the body: your brain already stacks the world in layers.

✜ Cortex first:
Sensory detail climbs into patterns, patterns into concepts, concepts into goals. No tiny king at the top; nested control spreads authority across layers. Once you track which signal overrides which impulse, you can also track rank: whose anger matters, whose approval changes your options.

✜ Rank in the skull:
Zink et al. taught volunteers a pecking order from faces alone. When rank rose or fell, the same reward networks that light up for money lit up in brain scans. Ten people can run on trust and direct talk; at company scale, the group trades detail for speed: one roll-up, one answer upstairs.

✜ Before the org chart:
Ten voices in a planning meeting become one VP bullet, trimmed by a director, shaped by whoever can block the roadmap. Your attention climbed the org chart before HR drew it.

Pyramids compress local realities into fewer steering points, then breed bottlenecks, status games, and the habit that you stop thinking past your layer.

Brains can rotate who leads by domain, run co-ops, and share roles instead of one fixed boss. Flatter systems need explicit counter-design: visible decisions, written process, norms that stop rank from silently returning. Without that, the room still orbits the founder or the mod with the delete button.

Hierarchy is an attractor, not destiny. We keep building pyramids because attention already climbs in layers.
