🎭 The deniability hood: why groups prefer not to know

In The Elephant in the Brain, Simler and Hanson argue that ignorance is most useful when it is most public. That sounds backwards: shouldn't you hide what you don't want known?

✜ The deniability hood: when a group adopts a clean public story ("safety," "tradition," "helping people"), everyone can see the gap and pretend to believe. Private suspicion never becomes the shared, undeniable fact that would trigger punishment.

✜ Plausible deniability: skirting a norm beats violating it outright. The pretext does not need to fool everyone. It only needs to block "everyone knows everyone knows." Challenge the story and you become the problem, not the liar.

✜ In trust games, plausible deniability pushed defection from 54% to 88%. Cover was enough for cooperative players to take the whole pie.

Shared public ignorance makes the whole group complicit. If everyone wears the hood, calling someone out exposes you too. Everyone suspects. Nothing moves until private doubt becomes common knowledge, the trigger that makes coalitions to punish possible.
