🧠 Why does a healthy brain forget your keys?

You walk into a room and forget why. You lose the keys. A name goes blank. After 40, that can feel like the start of Alzheimer's.

Lisa Genova's talk says those lapses are how a healthy memory works. The brain keeps what is meaningful, emotional, or repeated. It drops what you never paid attention to. A champion who memorized more than 111,000 digits of pi still forgot a birthday.

Names are hard because they are just labels. Remembering to do something later is worse: pilots use a checklist for the landing gear. Your phone steals the second that would have stored the parking spot. Looking the word up on Google does not wreck your memory.

Alzheimer's looks different. You fail to form new memories. You do not recognize your car. You do not know how you arrived. That clock can run quietly for 15 to 20 years. Sleep, walking, diet, new learning, and unmanaged chronic stress are the levers you actually have.

Everyday forgetting tracks attention. The disease tracks a slow plaque clock.
