🧬⚔️ War starts before anyone raises a flag

A chimpanzee community can live peacefully for decades, then split into factions and kill former allies. The scary part is what triggers it: network breakdown, the loss of mediators, status rivalry, and territorial pressure. No grand ideology required.

At Ngogo in Uganda, a group of ~200 chimps fractured after key connector figures died. Once enough bridges disappeared, "us" and "them" became easier to feel. The smaller faction attacked more often and won through tighter cohesion.

Ants push the point further. Argentine ant supercolonies fight months-long wars where millions die. Group boundaries, resource pressure, and payoffs that reward collective aggression do the job without belief, poetry, or politics.

✜ The baseline is darker than we like to admit. Organized lethal conflict can emerge before any flag, doctrine, or speechwriter arrives.

✜ Human wars look ideological at the surface, but the emotional runway starts earlier: fewer cross-group friendships, fewer trusted elders, more local grievance, more stories that make the other cluster feel disposable.

✜ Systems move when the most organized players have the clearest incentives. If aggression pays and restraint stays diffuse, the violent strategy gains ground.

The prevention lesson is boring and radical: cross-group friendships matter. Respected elders matter. Rituals of repair matter. Boring repeated contact matters.

Those things sound soft until they vanish. Once they vanish, the social graph rewires faster than people expect.

The flag comes late. The fracture starts earlier.
