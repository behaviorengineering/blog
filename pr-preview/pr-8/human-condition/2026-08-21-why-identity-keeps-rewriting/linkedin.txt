🪞 Why does the brain keep selling you a new "me"?

A stable "me" is a live mix. Heather Berlin, Ethan Kross, and Nicole Vignola argue the brain mixes what it already expects with what the senses pick up now, and that mix can shift when you change what you attend to and the story you tell about yourself.

✜ You pull the evidence your mood already wants
Mood decides what you notice, even when the kitchen has not changed. A negative headspace pulls more negative evidence. You live inside a perception box: a bounded window you treat as the whole world.

✜ The voice writes the plot, then it can loop
Your inner voice helps you plan and turn events into a story of who you are. When it loops as chatter (rumination and catastrophizing), you replay the same worry without solving anything.

✜ Self-criticism starts as copying
You copy what you see in parents and peers, not only what you are told. Bad news lands harder than good, so as an adult you still use those early stories.

Say the story they taught you about yourself, and the story you now tell yourself. Change what you attend to and which stories you repeat, and people who know you start meeting a different version of you.
