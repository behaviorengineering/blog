When the physics guru stops talking about the universe and talks about Vietnam

Michio Kaku spends most of a long podcast on multiverses and quantum hype. Then he tells a story from his army days that does not sound like a TED talk.

His sergeant had scars on half his face. The recruits asked why. A boy had walked up saying "candy, candy." It was a hand grenade. Half the sergeant's body took shrapnel.

Kaku's question is not how evil the world is. It is why a kid would do that. His answer: because he believed the story he was told, hard enough to turn a gift into a trap.

He still says you have to believe in the goodness of people and that people can do evil. You have to fight for what you think is right.

Every morning at 4 he sang a nasty marching song about killing "Charlie Kong." Then he wondered if he was on the right side or the wrong side.

That is the part that sticks. Fame does not spare you from asking which chorus you joined before breakfast.
