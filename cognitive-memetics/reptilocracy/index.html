W19: Public Money, Private Leverage
  • Reptilocracy
  • NarrativeControl
  • MisalignedIncentives
  • HiddenCosts
  • AccountabilityGap

✔️ TLDR

Tax money enters at one end and returns as lobbying power at the other. The public funds the loop, then faces the loop at the ballot box.

💬 Context

Incentives can build a closed loop between public funding, private advantage, and political influence. A concentrated group has time, expertise, and money to track rules, seek favorable treatment, and press for the next exception. The public cost spreads across many people, so each person has less reason and less capacity to follow every transfer.

Narrative control makes the loop sound technical: subsidy, partnership, competitiveness, reform. The result can look normal and professional even when public resources help finance the influence used to protect or expand private advantages. The smaller the group that gains, the louder it comes back to press again. Without transparent, independently overseen and auditable standards for honesty, accountability, and conflicts of interest, systems reward people who preserve the loop.

W18: Insert Vote, Receive Same
  • Reptilocracy
  • NarrativeControl
  • FalseChoice
  • AccountabilityGap
  • PsychFitness

✔️ TLDR

We can choose which candidate disappoints us, but not whether the system will. The vote changes the label while the machine keeps dispensing the same menu.

💬 Context

This scene shows narrative control and restricted choice. A large system can present many candidates, slogans, and campaigns while keeping the real decision range narrow. Short-term incentives, donor or status pressures, internal gatekeepers, and fear of upsetting established interests shape what reaches the ballot.

The room then treats dissatisfaction as a consumer preference problem: choose a different box next time. That makes recurring failure feel like the public chose badly. It is evidence that the menu itself is constrained. “If every option disappoints, the problem may be the machine.” Without basic psychological fitness standards for honesty, accountability, courage, and reality-contact (applied transparently and under independent oversight), systems keep rewarding candidates who fit the machine better than they serve the public.

W17: Charisma On, Empathy Off
  • Reptilocracy
  • NarrativeControl
  • FearChain
  • EmpathyGap
  • EmpathyExtraction

✔️ TLDR

On the table, they drain empathy out like excess fluid and tick the box for “success.” The checklist keeps charisma and party loyalty switched on, but turns off the part of us that hurts when others do.

💬 Context

This scene shows how fear chains and incentive structures frame empathy as a liability in high-stakes roles: feeling too much for those who bear the cost can slow decisions, resist harsh policies, or trigger guilt. Over time, the culture teaches us to separate public warmth from private feeling, so charm and slogans stay while real concern is numbed.

Narrative control then recasts emotional distance as “toughness,” “objectivity,” or “putting the mission first,” making the extracted empathy look like professional maturity. “When hurting less matters more than harming less, the system anesthetizes itself.” Without psychological fitness filters for genuine empathy, courage to act on it, and resistance to status-driven numbness, organizations promote leaders who can smile convincingly while their decisions quietly grind other nervous systems down.

W16: Photo-Op Readiness
  • Reptilocracy
  • AccountabilityGap
  • NarrativeControl
  • TransparencyTheatre
  • CalendarManagement

✔️ TLDR

Accountability is reduced to a calendar conflict.

💬 Context

We all know the rhythm. A corporation waits until the morning after a major product launch to announce mass layoffs. A politician schedules a ribbon-cutting ceremony just hours before a known scandal drops.

This is how modern institutions handle wrongdoing. They do not prevent the harm. They sequence it. Ethics simply becomes a branch of calendar management.

The panel targets this exact administrative approach to accountability. The primary concern is never the damage itself, but whether the damage intersects with a marketing event. Truth is treated as an operational risk, quietly held back until the cameras finally leave the room.

W15: Case Study 1
  • Reptilocracy
  • ComplianceTheatre
  • InstitutionalHypocrisy
  • WorkplaceBullying
  • NarrativeControl

✔️ TLDR

The people writing the rules of conduct are completely insulated from following them.

💬 Context

Every corporate worker and civil servant knows this exact dynamic. The HR department rolls out a mandatory, unskippable seminar on “respectful communication,” while the executive board runs entirely on intimidation, backstabbing, and screaming matches. A legislature drafts national anti-harassment laws inside a chamber that actively operates like a hostile work environment.

The panel targets this staggering lack of self-awareness. Power loves to enforce behavioral standards on the vulnerable, treating compliance as something the lower tiers must be taught.

But the rule-makers never see themselves in the training materials. They view ethics and conduct as administrative burdens for the public to absorb, completely oblivious to the fact that their own daily behavior is the perfect case study for everything the system claims to oppose.

W14: The False Fork
  • Reptilocracy
  • WhenChoiceBitesBack
  • FalseChoice
  • CollateralDamage
  • NarrativeControl

✔️ TLDR

The system gives you a choice between two bad outcomes, but only after forcing you to absorb the damage.

💬 Context

We see this structure everywhere. You are asked to vote for the lesser of two terrible candidates, but the act of participating validates the system that produced them both.

You are forced to choose between two healthcare plans that both deny you coverage, but only after paying the non-refundable premium to access the portal.

This panel targets the architecture of false choice. Institutions love to present the public with a fork in the road, demanding debate over which bad option is slightly less ruinous. But the debate is a distraction from the floor plan. The real cost isn’t what happens after you choose; it’s what you are forced to step over just to reach the turnstile.

By the time you pull the lever, the system has already taken what it wanted. The clerk in the booth doesn’t care which door you pick. They only care that you paid the entry fee.

W13: A Brighter Future
  • Reptilocracy
  • PromiseManagement
  • PublicMandate
  • NarrativeControl
  • AuthorizedBeneficiaries

✔️ TLDR

The public provides the mandate. The insiders take the exit.

💬 Context

We know the routine. A company demands wage freezes to secure a “brighter future,” then quietly pays out massive executive bonuses the moment the quarter ends.

A city passes a public levy to save local infrastructure, but the funds immediately vanish into private consultant fees. The public endures the sacrifice. The insiders collect the reward.

The panel targets this exact bait-and-switch. Political and corporate momentum is always built on the backs of a tired majority marching toward a promised light.

But the exit is never built for the crowd. The public simply provides the energy to keep the machine running, while the managers quietly step through a side door.

W11: Democracy — Annual Renewal
  • Reptilocracy
  • Democracy
  • SubscriptionEconomy
  • PoliticalRitual

✔️ TLDR

The public gets the ritual of choice and the cost of obedience. Consent becomes ceremony. Taxes become the receipt.

💬 Context

This panel turns democracy into a subscription you cannot cancel. The letter is polite, the language is clean, and that is what makes it ugly. Public will is not overturned with drama. It is processed, filed, and quietly replaced.

The renewal notice lists the insult in plain text: policies you opposed, included anyway; war, added as bonus content; payment, taken automatically. The joke lands because it feels familiar. People keep being told they live in a system of choice while opening invoice after invoice for decisions they never made.

What hurts is the calm of it. Nobody needs to shout. Nobody needs to hide the charge. The machine already knows that outrage fades faster than direct debit, and that most people are left paying for the opposite of what they asked for.

W11: If You Hold It, It Will Blow Up In Your Hand?
  • Reptilocracy
  • TransparencyTheatre
  • NarrativeControl
  • InformationLeaks
  • AccountabilityGap

✔️ TLDR

The transparency box stays sealed until something cracks and sprays information everywhere. We don’t get clean answers, just frantic scoops with buckets when the system bursts.

💬 Context

This scene shows narrative control around information: governments talk about openness while treating real transparency as a leak, a mistake, or a crime. Time horizons and fear chains make leaders more afraid of short-term embarrassment than of long-term rot, so they hide information until it escapes on its own.

The result feels chaotic rather than accountable, insiders manage damage while outsiders juggle partial truths. “When truth only comes out through cracks, trust erodes with it.” Without psychological fitness filters for honesty, accountability, and courage to face bad news early, systems reward people who patch the leaks instead of building glass walls.

W10: Bread And Circuses
  • Reptilocracy
  • SpectacleEconomy
  • ShortTermism
  • NarrativeControl
  • StructuralFixes

✔️ TLDR

The boring slide deck has working solutions, but the room turns toward the fireworks and clown car. We keep saying “no one has answers” while we ignore the ones that don’t sparkle.

💬 Context

This scene shows how time horizons and incentives push leaders to choose visible spectacle over quiet, structural fixes. Media cycles, polling, and internal status all reward moves that look exciting now, even if they don’t solve the underlying problem.

Narrative control helps by framing attention-grabbing stunts as strong leadership while labelling patient repair as naive, slow, or politically impossible. “We’re not out of ideas, just addicted to applause.” Without psychological fitness filters for honesty, long-term focus, and courage to back unglamorous work, systems keep promoting people who fund pyrotechnics and clown cars instead of actual solutions.
