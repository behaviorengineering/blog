🧠 Why does a healthy brain forget your keys?

Lost keys and a blank name after 40 feel like early Alzheimer's. Lisa Genova says a healthy brain was never built to catalog every name or parking spot. A memory champion who memorized 111,000 digits of pi still forgot a birthday.

✜ Distraction, not a broken store
You cannot retrieve what you never looked at. Encoding is the moment attention becomes a memory. Phones and feeds steal that second before the circuit starts. Googling a missing word leaves the store intact. Search is an open-book test, not digital amnesia.

✜ Names live in a cul-de-sac
Proper nouns have few routes in. A baker's job (apron, oven, smell) has thousands. The occupation sticks; the surname does not. Remembering to do something later fails by design. Pilots lower the wheels with a checklist.

✜ Lost keys vs lost arrival
Alzheimer's starts in the hippocampus and can run silently for 15 to 20 years. Normal forgetting is misplacing keys. The disease is failing to recognize your car, or not knowing how you arrived. For about 98% of people, sleep, walking, diet, new learning, and unmanaged chronic stress change whether sticky protein plaques hit a tipping point. Extra neural routes still work when one path is blocked.

Everyday forgetting tracks attention. Alzheimer's tracks a slow plaque clock.
