## Our brain does not even fit inside our brain

You **simplify** to think. You drop detail, draw a line around the problem, and accept a few small lies so you can hold it in your head. The **trap** is when you **forget** you made that cut and treat the slice as how reality works.

> Science helps us make sense of the world. Proving the world is simple was **never** the job.

## When *like* becomes *is*

Brain talk spreads in short comparisons: tubes, telegraph wires, computers, *prediction machines*. Each retelling drops what does not fit: **glia** (support cells in the brain, not the firing neurons), chemistry, bodies, everything outside the skull.

Repeat the slice often enough and the comparison **stops being a comparison**. The brain **gets sold as a computer**.

## The bench picks the metaphor

Your **mind-story** follows whatever tool is **on the bench**, the metaphor fashion of the moment. Today that tool is an **LLM** (a large language model trained on huge written archives).

The *"prediction engine"* brain story starts to sound like **fact** because the library it learned from comes back as smooth insight.

## Mechanism, story, or reassurance?

*"How does the brain work?"* sounds simple. **It is not one question.**

The answer depends on whether you need a **mechanism**, a fundable **story**, a product roadmap, or **reassurance**. **AGI** hype (artificial general intelligence, sold as almost here) narrows the question to *"are we on track?"* and **benchmarks** that sound certain until the model leaves training and stumbles.

Before you repeat a frame, say what it is **for**, and what it **leaves out**.
