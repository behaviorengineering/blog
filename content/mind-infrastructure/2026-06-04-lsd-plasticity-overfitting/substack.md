## A story that stayed with me for years

When I was at uni, one of my housemates told me a story that sat in my brain for years. He had a friend who would show up to **one class on LSD every time**. No notebook, no laptop, yet he supposedly **passed every exam**.

Now that psychedelic research has gone **mainstream**, this story got my attention again in a different way. Classic psychedelics like LSD can open a brief **neuroplasticity window**: a state where the brain **rewires synapses** more readily for **hours or even days** after a dose.

## What the studies actually show

A **low-dose LSD study**, testing people **the next day**, found improved **episodic memory** and **verbal fluency**, but reduced **cognitive flexibility** on tasks where the **rules changed without warning**.

![Infographic from an LSD dose: three arrows for memory improvement (enhanced episodic recall), verbal fluency (faster word retrieval), and cognitive flexibility (reduced rule-switching on tasks like the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test)](lsd-cognitive-effects.webp)

Scientific reviews confirm the pattern: after one dose, **plasticity genes**, **dendrite morphology**, and **synaptic strength** shift, with effects that last beyond the **acute experience**. That fits a **short plasticity window**, not a permanent rewire. Human signals are noisier, but **the trend is clear**.

## Better memory, worse adaptation

That next-day mix looks a lot like **overfitting** in machine learning: **recent inputs carry too much weight**, which spikes recall but sinks the ability to **adapt to new rules**. Call it a temporary bias toward **whatever is directly in front of you**; you trade sharper memory for **flexibility** to update your mental model.

> No paper I cite uses *overfitting* for LSD cognition; this is an **analogy**, not established neuroscience.

## What that would mean for the story

With that frame, my housemate's story stops being **pure magic**. If you **repeat the same lecture** with a brain that is extra plastic and **almost obsessively** focused on what is in front of you, you might encode those classes with **unusual strength**.

I never verified the anecdote, and this is **not a recommendation**. It is a **speculative** read on why note-less exam stories feel less impossible, not proof they happened.
