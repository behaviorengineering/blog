## When clarity turns into chaos

You put a date or an intent on the table, and the talk does **not** resolve. It spins into **chaos**, while the fight you meant to finish sits under old complaints and stories that keep changing shape.

This is less a normal disagreement than **two ways** of seeing the same scene.

> One side thinks facts **clear** things up; the other treats that **clarity** as something to **dodge**.

Palmatier calls the shared habit **character pathology**: how someone handles reality and blame. Clinic labels often miss half the behavior or over-label the person; this name is useful because it points at the **pattern** first. When the habit is *I will not face the record*, facts will **not** close the argument, and the stated dispute is mostly **cover**.

## When a fact feels like invasion

In a normal conflict, a fact clears what is unknown. Here it leaves a **trail** of broken promises, double standards, and rewritten stories. For a **false self** (the polished image that cannot survive a mistake), that trail is dangerous: an inconvenient continuity for a story that needs distortion.

They need their version taken as true. When a fact in time proves the error, the floor drops out, and they attack whoever put them on the record.

**Emotional reasoning** takes over: *"I feel attacked by the facts, so you must be attacking me."* They use feelings as **proof** and bend reality around that sensation. Your tone becomes the *"evidence,"* your intent disappears, and they may rewrite what you said, attribute their own defects to you (**gaslighting**, **projection**), and drop the facts to focus on their reaction.

## Rage protects the narrative

Rage at an irrefutable fact is usually fear of **losing control** of the narrative, not guilt over a detail. The cleaner the evidence, the hotter the response.

They change the subject, rewrite last month, question your motives, or run you in circles until you lose the thread. The loop only needs to last long enough for **accountability** to dissolve into a fog of complexity.

## The cost of "peace"

You match pieces and dates; they play at building a negotiable reality where truth is optional. If you explain, you *"attack."* If you ask for accountability, you *"control."* Every valid clarification becomes material for the next rewrite of the facts.

When facts keep triggering rage, *"better communication"* will **not** help. If you cannot agree what happened, you cannot move on.

> Better to **avoid contact** and set limits than burn time on heroic conversations.

Any bond that asks you to drop checkable facts for *"peace"* is already broken. That *"peace"* which denies reality does **not** hold.
