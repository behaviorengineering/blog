### The collapse of the shared timeline

You try to pin down a date or an intent, and the talk does not resolve. It spins into **chaos**, while the fight you meant to finish sits under a stack of old complaints and stories that keep changing shape.

What you are watching is less a normal disagreement than two ways of seeing the same scene. One side thinks facts clear things up; the other treats that kind of clarity as something to dodge.

Palmatier calls the shared habit **character pathology**: how someone handles reality and blame. Clinic labels often miss half the behavior or over-label the person, so this name is useful because it points at the pattern first. When the habit is *I will not face the record*, facts will not close the argument, and the stated dispute is mostly cover.

### When a fact feels like invasion

In ordinary fights, facts shrink the unknown. Here they leave a **trail** of broken promises, double standards, and story shifts. For a **false self** (the polished image that cannot survive a mistake), that trail is dangerous, because it forces continuity where the preferred story needed a blank slate.

They need their version to stay on top. When a timeline proves them wrong, the floor drops out, and you become the aggressor for pointing at the bars of the cage.

To keep the preferred story standing, **emotional reasoning** takes over: *I feel attacked by the facts, therefore you are attacking me.* Feeling is treated as proof, reality bends around the feeling, and your tone starts counting as "evidence" while your intent disappears. They may rewrite what you said, dump their own moves onto you (**gaslighting**, **projection**), and pull the argument off what happened and onto how they feel about what happened.

### Rage protects the story

Rage at a hard fact is usually about **losing grip** on the shared story, not guilt over a detail. The cleaner the evidence, the hotter the response has to get.

They change the subject, rewrite last month, and question your motives, or run you in circles until you cannot keep the thread. The loop only has to last long enough that **accountability** never lands.

### The cost of "peace"

You are matching pieces and dates while they play a different game, one where reality stays negotiable and facts stay optional. Explain, and you are "attacking." Ask for accountability, and you are "controlling." Every clean clarification becomes raw material for the next rewrite.

When facts keep triggering rage, better wording will not save the dynamic. If you cannot agree what happened, you cannot move on, so the workable path looks more like **low contact** and cool, work-style limits than another heroic conversation. Any bond that requires you to drop checkable facts for "peace" is already broken. Peace bought by denying reality does not hold.
