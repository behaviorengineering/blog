## Why does purpose change your mitochondria?

Energy is **real**. Most of us feel it as the gap between having the agency to change our lives and barely being through the day.

Dr. **Martin Picard** argues that your body runs on a **fixed energy budget**. Extra food refills the pool, but the daily budget stays fixed. When infection, threat, or rumination flares, repair, brain maintenance, and anti-aging work have to wait in line.

> Picard hears **budget trouble** at least as often as mood trouble.

## Ancient bacteria still run the show

About 1.5 billion years ago, an oxygen-using bacterium merged with a larger host cell, and the deal stuck. You carry about **five quadrillion mitochondria** (roughly a thousand per cell). They turn food and oxygen into electricity, heat, and signals.

These are **social cells** that have outsourced their metabolic work to symbionts. You are **consortia**. The "you" that cares about purpose rides on bacteria that negotiated their place before animals existed.

## The stress that drains you is your response

The crucial distinction is between **stress** and the **stress response**. You can read a worrying email and your body acts as if you already lost the contract. Cortisol rises, shoulders tighten, and the budget still drains hours after the threat has passed.

In lab tests, human cells exposed to stress signals raised their energy spend by **60%**. They aged faster without any physical damage—just the signal that danger might be outside.

The evidence of these budget shocks is recorded in your hair. Much like **tree rings**, drought years show in the rings, and a strand of hair keeps a faster record. The scalp holds a timeline of when your cellular budget went **overdrawn**.

## Purpose as a magnet for energy

Does **purpose** improve mitochondrial function? Research on postmortem brain tissue suggests a stronger **sense of purpose** correlates with more efficient mitochondria.

Picard treats worthwhile goals as **magnets for energy**. When purpose feels foggy and your body feels spent, it is often a matter of **energy allocation**. Worthwhile goals help pull spend back into one channel.
