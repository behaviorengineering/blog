### Your body runs on a fixed budget

**Energy is real.** Most of us feel it as the gap between having the agency to change our lives and barely getting through the day. The **energy resistance** model treats the body as a **fixed energy budget** that decides what gets paid first. Extra food refills the pool; the **budget** stays fixed. When infection, threat, or rumination flares, repair, pigment, brain maintenance, and anti-aging work wait in line.

### Ancient bacteria still run the show

About 1.5 billion years ago, an oxygen-using bacterium merged with a larger host cell, and the deal stuck. You carry on the order of **five quadrillion mitochondria** (roughly a thousand per cell). They turn food and oxygen into electricity, heat, and signals.

These are **social cells** that have outsourced their metabolic work to symbionts. Symbiosis bought division of labor, then organs, then brains. You are **consortia**. The "you" that cares about purpose rides on bacteria that negotiated their place before animals existed.

### The stress that drains you is your response

The crucial distinction is between **stress** and the **stress response**. You can read a worrying email and your body acts as if you already lost the contract, cortisol up, shoulders tight, the worst case on loop at midnight, and the budget still drains hours after the threat has passed.

His students ran the mechanism in a dish. Human fibroblasts, glucocorticoid added, no physical damage, just the hormonal signal that danger might be outside. Energy expenditure rose about **60%**. The cells aged faster. That figure comes from dish biology, not your whole daily burn, yet faster heart rate, tight muscles, and rumination at 2 a.m. still drain the **budget**.

Hard exercise works differently: acute load, recovery, and a payoff in more mitochondria and better **energy resistance**, how well cells handle demand.

The evidence of these budget shocks is even recorded in your hair. Much like tree rings, drought years still show in the rings, just like a strand of hair keeps a faster record. In his **eLife** study, just fourteen people, white bands along the shaft lined up with stressful stretches of life. When stress dropped soon enough, some color came back. The scalp holds a timeline of when your cellular budget went overdrawn.

### Purpose concentrates the flow

Does **purpose** improve mitochondrial function? Postmortem prefrontal tissue from hundreds of older adults (**Trumpff et al., PNAS 2024**) suggests it might. A stronger **sense of purpose** correlated with more efficient mitochondria. Purpose was one item in a wider psychosocial score, and the study shows association, not a switch you flip in your cells.

Worthwhile goals act as **magnets for energy**. When mental load stays high, **GDF15**, a protein that signals energetic deficit, tends to rise. Depression, burnout, and flat motivation sit on that same curve when energetic deficit comes first.

### Focus on the spend

You do not need every disease chapter to see the pattern. Ayurveda, qigong, and traditional Chinese medicine tracked **balance, flow, blockage, and depletion** before modern biology named the mechanisms. The most effective levers are not supplement shortcuts, but **exercise, sleep, mindful eating, and social connection**.

Swap the question "How do I get more energy?" for **where is my budget going** and **what is worth the spend**. When purpose feels foggy and your body feels spent, it is often **budget** trouble at least as often as mood trouble. His **energy resistance** take on cancer and Alzheimer's is still debated.
