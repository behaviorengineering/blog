## Why steal pears you will not eat?

St. Augustine stole pears from an orchard, then threw them to the pigs. **No hunger.** **No profit.** Just the itch to do something **wrong** because it was wrong.

Paul Bloom calls that itch **perversity**: the strange, human drive to break a rule *simply because it is a rule*. Not a lapse. Not a hidden prize. A sharp claim that you are still **unmanageable**.

> Some breaks in the rules exist only to prove we **can**.

## Tiny strikes, same itch

The same move shows up in petty corners. Finger in the ice cream. A pointless flirt that tests whether your **agency** still belongs to you.

These are small, unmotivated strikes against the expected order. They remind you that you are **not** a biological machine following a script. Even when the act is useless, it is a private signal: *my will is still mine*.

## When the map gets too tight

Push too hard to script people and the itch scales up. During COVID, vaccine mandates triggered a wave of **reactance**: refusal aimed at the *requirement itself*, because it felt like a grab at sovereignty.

When a system gets too predictable or too demanding, the impulse is to move the other way on purpose. You choose the less rational path to protect the territory of your own decisions.

## Shred the auction

In culture, the same perversity can rewrite the game. Banksy's *Girl with Balloon* shredded itself at auction: a calculated strike against the art market's script that changed the work's meaning and raised its value.

Doing the *"wrong"* thing can be a sophisticated way to force a new reality into the room.

## Unmanageable, or aimed?

Your small rebellions sit in the same loop. Autonomy is **vital**. Pure **reactance** is not a plan. You can end up defined by what you **refuse**, not by what you **want**.

Look at your last act of defiance. Was it a genuine move toward a goal, or just a way to feel unmanageable in front of a rule?

*"What question can break the autonomy-perversity cycle?"*
