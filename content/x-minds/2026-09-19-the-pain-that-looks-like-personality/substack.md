## A period in a text can hit like a fist

A partner drops a **period** instead of an emoji, and **shame** hits your chest like a physical blow. Heart racing. Mind collapsing into *worthlessness*. That spike has a name: **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD)**, intense emotional and physical pain after real or perceived rejection, criticism, or teasing.

The **brake** that should slow the surge is a missing **inhibitory signal**. Without that neurological **pause**, every slight can accelerate into a full landslide.

> The pain **looks like** personality. The mechanism is a **biological gap**.

## When the breakup gets labeled as character

A teenager spirals into self-harm after a devastating breakup. Clinicians apply a **personality disorder** label, and the shame gets heavy.

When underlying **ADHD** comes into view, the story shifts. The intensity tracks a lack of inhibitory signals in the **prefrontal cortex**. This is a brain without a natural brake on emotional intensity, **not** a permanent verdict on who someone is.

Understanding the mechanism replaces shame with a pragmatic focus: manage a **biological reality**.

## Wrong meds when the pause is missing

People bounce through **treatment-resistant** depression or anxiety while standard antidepressants never quite land. Often the primary struggle is a **dopamine** and **noradrenaline** deficiency dressed up as a mood story.

Spot **ADHD-linked RSD** and options like **Guanfacine** become available. That medication boosts noradrenergic activity to create an emotional **pause** and calm an overactive fear response. When the spikes finally quiet, you see a specific **neurochemical gap**.

## Name the spike before the weekend burns

A vague *"let's talk Monday"* from a boss can erase an entire weekend. Catastrophic thinking is a hallmark of RSD: the brain fails to rationalize in real time.

Recovery starts with labeling. Say *"this is my RSD"* and you open a conscious **gap**. In that gap, best-case and realistic outcomes can land before the landslide takes the whole day.

## Build the pause on purpose

Stop asking if your reactions prove you are **unstable**. Ask whether you get even **one second** between trigger and spike. A landslide that lands before the thought registers points to a neurological lack of **inhibition**.

When the next wave of shame or rage hits, name it immediately. That label draws a boundary between **identity** and the biological surge. It supplies the small, artificial pause your cortex needs to start thinking again.
