🚽 Would you ask permission in a meeting if you had to go to the toilet?

At school, a child who needs the bathroom asks an adult first. The bell decides when children move. Teachers reward the child who waits quietly, and that posture later feels normal in meetings, factories, and office desks.

✜ School trains compliance apart from learning. A child can follow the schedule, sit still, and accept a grade without understanding the work. The system rewards the behavior that makes groups easy to manage.

✜ Factory managers saw the same problem before mass schooling had reached every worker. Cotton-mill doffers did the same job on the same machines in 1910, yet productivity varied sixfold. In some regions, one worker tended six machines. In others, a worker tended one. Managers blamed workers who resisted direct orders, arrived late, or refused tasks that clashed with status.

✜ School also teaches people to see rank as fair. Most fifth graders still want equal shares. By late adolescence, most prefer to divide rewards according to individual achievement.

The benefits are real. Schools can teach manners, cooperation, and quieter ways to settle conflict. They can also train people to submit to a modern hierarchy, while curiosity pays the bill.
