## When a "prediction" is really a prescription

Carissa Véliz (Oxford) on *The Tea* with Myriam François: about **69 minutes** on **surveillance**, **democracy**, and why many algorithmic lines read like weather but **act like commands**. If you only skim headlines about **existential AI doom**, you can miss the threats that already landed: **privacy**, **accountability**, and **anonymous protest**.

> A forecast you cannot refuse is a push dressed up as foresight.

## Cheaper surveillance than East Germany

East Germany's Stasi was **expensive and human-heavy**: it could watch roughly a third of the population. Today's stack is different. Phones, browsers, and platforms collect **thousands of signals per person**, cheaply, across whole populations.

Mass data buys **prediction and influence**: who will click, vote, default, or protest. That is power over your **next move**, not just ad revenue.

## "Nothing to hide" protects you from power

Véliz flips the comfortable line. **Privacy limits what powerful actors can infer and do** to you and to people connected to you.

You already hide your PIN without feeling guilty. The same logic applies at scale: data is often collected **far beyond** what a service needs (sexual preference data to sell handbags is her blunt example). The risk is **abuse of power later**, not proof of wrongdoing today.

**Palantir-style** state tooling and everyday apps that **nudge your next click** build **soft authoritarian** infrastructure: convenience hides who steers you.

## Scores and prophecy are bets you live inside

**AI scores** for hiring, loans, and health look like neutral forecasts. They are **bets on your behavior** that institutions execute. A model that "predicts" you will underperform is not a passive description; it is a **move that shapes what happens next** (you do not get the interview).

Executive prophecy (*"you will all use AI tomorrow"*) works the same way: it recruits the public to **fulfill** someone's preferred timeline. Véliz calls that combo **obedience in advance**.

Models trained on the **past** can miss **systemic risk** (her *turkey* fable in the talk: confidence rises until the crash the training set never showed).

## When prediction becomes a shield

Headlines about superintelligence distract from medical scores, chatbots, and **responsibility games**. COVID saw dozens of medical AI tools deployed without proper validation; meta-analyses later found many useless or harmful. Early cancer detection sounds great until **overdiagnosis** and false positives dominate the tradeoff.

The **Air Canada** chatbot case: wrong grief-fee advice, then the company tried to treat the bot as a **separate legal speaker**. Courts pushed back. The pattern: predictions and bots become **shields** when institutions want to deny responsibility.

**Resistance** in the talk is practical: **privacy tools**, **skepticism**, and **time offline** (analog life), not a moral slogan about the best outcome for many.
