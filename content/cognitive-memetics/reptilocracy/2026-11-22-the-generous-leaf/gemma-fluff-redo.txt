FLUFF:
The ants stand in a silent, hungry line with their empty baskets, waiting to ask the clerk how much gold the new statue of the leaf requires, while the crocodile’s sudden hunger is filed away as nothing more than a passing change in the weather. Because impulse control was never a line item on the morning agenda, the committee spends three hours arguing about which marble pedestal will best capture the profound generosity of a single scrap of vegetation.

The clerk looks up from the mountain of paperwork to authorize the unveiling permit with a heavy, rhythmic thud of his stamp, ignoring the fact that the predator’s appetite is the only thing currently filling the room. No one mentions the missing harvest or the lack of a psychological fitness review, because the meeting is far too busy debating the aesthetic merits of a monument that turns a theft into a miracle.

By the time the next season arrives, the theft has been successfully reclassified as a seasonal gift, a transition managed by the same bureaucracy that ensures the continuity of the spectacle. The crocodile’s mouth stays green, the clerk keeps stamping, and the empty stores are simply the price of celebrating a leader who occasionally remembers to leave a crumb behind.
