## The border fight is not where culture breaks

We keep hearing that immigrants are **destroying culture**, that things changed because the **street sounds different**. That story only lands where culture is already **thin**. **It breaks when human ties die**, not when a line on the map moves.

## Queen and trail in your pocket

At some point, daily life starts to look less like a culture and more like an **ant colony**. The **feed** plays **queen**; your **pocket** drops the **pheromones**. Millions react to the same cues, follow invisible trails, consume the same inputs, and think the idea is theirs.

> While people argue about outsiders, something quieter happens at the table. **Culture thins out.**

## When culture still had roots

Look at **Venezuela** in its boom years. It absorbed waves of immigrants from Europe, the Middle East, and across Latin America. People did not erase what was there. They **adapted** to it. A **strong culture pulls others in**.

In many industrialized societies today, culture does not grow from daily life. It gets **streamed into people's heads**. Movies, media, and **algorithmic feeds** repeat the same patterns until they feel natural.

## The factory moved into your pocket

The **industrial education system** did this at scale: same schedules, same lessons, same mental tracks. It trained people to **move together and think in sync**. Now that system runs in your **pocket**, all day long.
