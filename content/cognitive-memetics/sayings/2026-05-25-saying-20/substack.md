## "El diablo sabe más por viejo que por diablo." (The devil knows more from being old than from being the devil.)

Ever hear a Venezuelan elder drop this when someone sharp but green just missed the obvious?

## Not because he's the devil

The devil gets credit for two things: his **infamous name**, and his **long life**. The punch line lands on the second one. **Time on the clock** outranks **innate trickiness**. It is not a boast about evil genius. It is a quiet bet on **mileage** and **pattern recognition**.

Closest English cousins include *Experience is the best teacher*, *There is no substitute for experience*, and *You can't buy experience*. Same family, same shrug.

## When you hear it

You reach for it when a grandparent cuts through a shortcut with one line, or when the clever newcomer loses to **tenure** and **patience**. **Experience** closes a deal that **talent** tried to rush.

## What it really weighs

The line carries **warm respect** for people who earned their read on the world. Not debate trophies. Not the mask. **Years lived** outweigh **a reputation for mischief**.

> The horns are the joke. **Old age** is the proof.
